Macedonian Headlines

Electricity price and ESM loan

The government will again guarantee a new €60 million bank loan for state power producer ESM; bids for the new household electricity price open on 27 Nov.

Alta Banka case

Parliamentary oversight hearing continues. PM Mickoski blames the previous government for letting the Serbian bank in; SDSM filed an interpellation against central bank governor Trajko Slaveski.

EU path and the Bulgaria dispute

Mickoski, after the Brussels EU–Western Balkans summit, expects the full Growth Plan funds to be released and says the country has met its Reform Agenda obligations. He says he is open to talks with Sofia and Brussels but will not accept constitutional changes without guarantees and a minorities Action Plan first; he calls the constitutional requirement a blocker for starting accession talks.

BTA · Albeu

Mickoski says the reshuffle of second- and third-tier government posts should finish by the end of November; ZNAM leader Maksim Dimitrievski hinted he might leave over a bar-exam-in-Albanian dispute, but the PM calls the government stable. Two planned laws would let couples up to age 35 buy homes 20% below the local market price. The Albanian flag will again be displayed in Skopje and several municipalities on 28 November.

Geopolitics Facts – Oceans, Straits & Chokepoints

  1. The Yellow Fleet: When the Suez Canal closed in the 1967 Six-Day War, 15 ships were trapped in the Great Bitter Lake; they stayed until 1975, eight years.
  2. Sound Dues: Denmark taxed ships passing the Øresund from the 1420s until the 1857 Copenhagen Convention ended the toll for a lump-sum payment from maritime states.
  3. Panama's lake in the middle: Ships crossing the Panama Canal are lifted about 26 m by locks to Gatún Lake, which was the largest man-made lake in the world when the canal opened in 1914.
  4. Strait of Magellan: Ferdinand Magellan's fleet found this passage in 1520; it long served as the main route between the Atlantic and Pacific before the Panama Canal.
  5. Bosphorus: It narrows to roughly 700 m between the fortresses of Rumeli Hisarı and Anadolu Hisarı, built in the 1390s and 1450s respectively.
